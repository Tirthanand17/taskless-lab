# Branch 3 — Evidence-led clipping-channel research
Research date: **2026-11-12**. This is platform policy/strategy research, **not** legal advice or a promise of virality.

## Primary official evidence
1. YouTube monetization/reused-content policy: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392
   - Clips, compilations, commentary and reaction videos can be monetizable **only if** they add substantial, discernible original value; superficial edits and mass-produced reused videos are risky.
   - Permission, fair use and monetization review are **separate** questions.
2. YouTube copyright: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797466
   - Reuse with permission, compliant Creative Commons license, public domain or qualifying exception may be possible; none guarantees no Content ID claim/strike.
3. YouTube fair use guidance: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148
   - Fact-specific, ultimately court-decided; not an automatic defense based on length, attribution or disclaimers. Laws differ by country.
4. YouTube licenses: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797468
   - Default standard license is **not** an invitation to reupload. CC BY needs correct attribution and rights investigation.
5. YouTube copyright strikes: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000
   - Three active strikes in 90 days can threaten associated channels; Content ID claim is not the same as a strike.
6. YouTube Content ID: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370
   - Automated matches can block, track or redirect monetization. Passing automated checks does not establish rights.
7. YouTube Shorts: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877
   - Up to 3 minutes. Third-party-claimed Shorts **over 1 minute** are blocked worldwide while active claim persists. A shorter clip is **not** automatically copyright safe.
8. YouTube Shorts analytics: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217
   - Watch **shown in feed**, **chose to view vs swiped**, audience retention, likes, comments, subscribers.
9. YouTube view measurement, Aug 2026: https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/
   - First-frame views vs engaged views; do not optimize for inflated superficial views.
10. YouTube original Shorts priority (TeamYouTube community post, 2026-10-01):
    https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts
    - Original perspective, commentary and creative storytelling receive more emphasis than unmodified aggregation.
11. Channel creation: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1646861
    - Google-account owner interaction needed. Channel creation cannot be completed by YouTube Data API video-publishing methods.

## This channel's stricter-than-law editorial rule
**Only use clips from creators who expressly authorize clipping.** A direct written rights-holder grant or a public creator-published clipping policy with evidence, identity, scope, duration, monetization and attribution must be reviewed before any acquisition or editing. Even if a particular use might qualify under copyright exceptions or CC BY, this project will not use it without the creator's explicit clipping permission. Public permission is not enough if third-party music/footage is excluded. Permission is different from YPP eligibility and cannot guarantee no claim. See https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 and https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797449.

## Choice of format
**Positioning:** “The interesting moment, explained.” Use 1–2 licensed short excerpts from ONE source in each educational story, not a compilation of unrelated celebrities.
**Content pillars:** mindset & decision-making; science/technology explained; creator/business lessons; fascinating interviews and surprising ideas. Start with the strongest two pillars based on source rights and viewer response.
**Main Short test length:** 25–55 seconds as an initial editorial hypothesis, not a YouTube algorithm requirement.
**Editorial arc:** 0–2s original hook → setup/context → short permissioned excerpt → original visual breakdown/response → clear actionable takeaway and specific question. Add source episode link, creator attribution and any required licensed credit.
**Avoid:** copied intros, misleading quotes, invented guest endorsement, repeatedly recycled same excerpt, excessive effects, inaccessible captions, fake “podcast facts,” copyrighted background music without separate rights, unapproved faces/voices, and copyright-dispute automation.

## 30-day learning plan (only AFTER owner creates channel and permissions exist)
- Weeks 1–2: test 3 topic pillars and 2 hook treatments; publish at a sustainable cadence such as 1 Short/day **if** licensed clips are approved. Never schedule unapproved clips.
- Weeks 3–4: use channel analytics to compare matched topics/lengths/time windows. Inspect viewed-vs-swiped, engaged views, average percentage viewed, relative retention curve, shares, comments quality and subscribers per engaged view.
- Use rolling baselines/medians and sample sizes; do not declare a hook successful from 1 viral outlier.
- For each source creator, maintain license and attribution requirements plus opt-out/takedown contact.
- Full 2–5-minute explainers can be a later format if enough original analysis exists; do not treat them as pure podcasts reuploads.

## Research conclusion
A direct “viral podcast clip repost farm” has significant copyright, YPP and recommendation-distribution risk. Stronger long-term strategy is authorized **transformative context clips** with meaningful new reporting/storytelling. This still cannot guarantee rights clearance, monetization or reach.
