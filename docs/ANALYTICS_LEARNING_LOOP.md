# Analytics Learning Loop

## Purpose

Do not let the automation become a content factory that repeats weak ideas. Publishing data must change the next episode.

## Review windows

Record metrics after:
- 24 hours;
- 72 hours;
- 7 days.

## Shorts decision rules

Track:
- Shorts-feed impressions / shown in feed when available;
- stayed to watch vs swiped away;
- average view duration;
- average percentage viewed;
- likes and comments;
- subscribers gained.

Interpretation:
- Low stayed-to-watch → first frame/hook problem.
- Good hook but early retention drop → payoff is too slow.
- High average percentage viewed but few impressions → keep the concept; test packaging/topic, do not blindly reupload.
- Comments asking the same question → create the next episode around that question.

## Long-form decision rules

Track:
- impressions;
- CTR;
- first 30-second retention;
- average view duration;
- average percentage viewed;
- top moments, spikes and dips;
- subscribers gained.

Interpretation:
- Low CTR + solid retention → packaging problem.
- Strong CTR + weak first 30 seconds → promise/hook mismatch.
- Spike → viewers replayed or shared that moment; make it easier to reach and consider a Short.
- Dip → remove or tighten similar sections in future episodes.

## Experiment log

Every episode should record:
- title variant used;
- cover/thumbnail variant used;
- hook pattern;
- duration;
- pacing pattern;
- CTA;
- results at each review window.

## Stop conditions

Do not increase volume merely because automation makes publishing cheap. Reduce cadence if:
- retention declines across three consecutive uploads;
- episodes become materially repetitive;
- comments indicate synthetic/low-effort perception;
- privacy or factual-quality gates fail.
